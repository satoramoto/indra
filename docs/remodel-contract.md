# Remodel contract

This is the shared interface for the Developer, Scheduler, Product, Surfaces and Migration lanes. `docs/mission.md` is the behavior target. Contract code adds boundaries and no-op Product/migration entry points; it does not implement their loops. No live state or runtime checkout is migrated by this PR.

## Explicit readiness and durable state

A team opts in with `workflowModel: "goals-v1"`. `parseState` then requires exactly one Team Lead, one Product and at least one Developer. Historical two-role teams omit the discriminator and cannot contain Product. The agreed Product identity is George Duke, `seat-002`; changing the role later must preserve his ID, display name and Mattermost identity. Nothing here assigns a role or invents a credential, home channel or project.

New-model `PlanningGoal` records also have `workflowModel: "goals-v1"`, `ownedFiles`, and an optional `goalProposal: ProductProposal`. The existing `proposal` and `assignments` fields remain exclusively historical. `goalProposal` contains its own proposal ID, Product seat provenance, positive rank, numbered outcomes, rationale, mission/retro references and proposed scope. The Team Lead may correct it before publication. Unpublished proposals are business drafts in the runtime Product queue; they are not PlanningGoal records and require no fabricated post IDs. Product posts only the chosen next proposal, then calls `PlanningStore.publishProductProposal(teamId, proposal, {id,userId,channelId,rootId,createdAt}, vetting)` with its actual root post. This method binds the state home/project and Product identity and atomically creates the durable proposal-stage goal. Published proposals/scope are immutable so a previously delivered reaction cannot approve rewritten content; refinement applies to unpublished entries. Approval requires a nonempty goal scope exactly matching that proposal, native ceremony evidence and an authenticated human. Approved scope, proposal, goal identity, project and Team Lead become immutable.

`PlanningStore.approveGoal(id, ApprovalEvidence, at?)` uses the same ceremony approval validation as historical proposal approval; a reaction adapter must GET-verify a human and the matching delivered proposal before calling it. New-model bridge approval GET-verifies the Product-authored root post; the owner may react on that root or use `planning approve`. Chick does not need to own or edit Product’s post. Team-seat reactions are rejected again by state reference validation. The goal can remain approved without any assignment or sprint branch. `PlanningStore.assignGoal(id, seatId, integration, at?)` atomically records `goalAssignment: { seatId, status: "assigned" | "running" | "reported" | "failed", updatedAt }` plus the `sprint/<goal-id>` integration. Only an idle Developer is eligible. New assignments reject unclosed legacy goals and overlapping active scopes, including across teams sharing a project. A reported Developer may take another disjoint goal while release finishes. Assigned scopes remain reserved until ceremony closure.

New queues may contain multiple unassigned or disjoint active goals. Legacy records never acquire new-model eligibility by inference. Native state history stays append-only, and new records start in planning. Historical approval/assignment/ceremony records remain readable without fabricated owned files, Product provenance or approvals.

## Strict agent output shapes

`src/goal-contract.ts` exports TypeScript shapes and strict unknown-input validators. JSON output schemas are `schemas/goal-brief.json`, `goal-report.json`, `product-proposal.json`, and `lane-plan.json`. Every object field is required, every object rejects extra properties, and a nullable field represents an intentional absence. The existing `schemas/brief.json` remains the historical clarification-response schema.

`GoalBrief` has version, goal/team/seat identity, header (`repo`, `baseBranch`, immutable `baseSha`, `branch`, `prTarget`), consecutively numbered outcomes with description/reason/current-code pointers, owned files, exclusions with owners and reasons, swarm instruction, up to the three available recent retros, redirect messages with source IDs and time, and the report-format instruction. A goal brief targets main from its own sprint. A lane brief branches from that sprint and targets it. Do not fabricate a third retro when fewer exist.

`GoalReport` has version, goal/team/seat identity, its exact sprint branch and immutable head, lane PR proofs (`laneId`, URL, head/merge SHA, reviewer `satori-miyamoto`, CI `passed`), exact commands with exit codes, decisions, follow-ups, and needed-but-unowned files. The parser preserves failed check results; successful delivery requires zero exit codes and no needed-but-unowned work, plus independent GitHub verification. Schema-valid claims alone never establish merge/review/CI facts. Ceremony implementation may freeze the verified report as delivery evidence; mutable lane state, event cursors, sessions and usage remain runtime-only.

`LanePlan` has version, goal ID, file-disjoint lanes (`id`, branch, owned files, dependencies) and nullable contract-lane ID. Dependencies must exist and be acyclic. A declared contract lane has no dependencies and every other lane transitively follows it. `validateLanePlan(plan, approvedOwnedFiles)` also enforces each lane's containment within the approved goal. Use that two-argument form before dispatch.

`ProductProposal` has version, goal/proposal IDs, Product seat ID, positive rank, mission, summary, numbered outcomes, owned files, risks, rationale and retro references. Product's unapproved queue cap is five across unpublished candidates plus any published unapproved goal, counted by `assertProductQueueCapacity` without double-counting goal IDs. State permits only one outstanding published Product proposal per team. An approved goal leaves the unapproved queue and belongs to the Scheduler. Before publication, Scheduler emits `proposal-vetted` carrying `ProductProposalVetting` (`proposalId`, `leadSeatId`, corrected `ownedFiles`, notes, time). Product accepts a result only for that exact candidate revision, updates its unpublished candidate scope and stores the vetting alongside it. Publication checks the actual Team Lead and exact corrected scope. A further candidate edit requires new vetting; changing published scope requires a new proposal and new human approval, never editing the sealed record.

## File boundaries

`validateOwnedFiles`, `ownedFileMatches`, `ownedFilesOverlap`, `assertOwnedFilesWithin`, and `assertDisjointOwnedFiles` operate on path languages, not the current checkout. Supported syntax is repository-relative literals, `*`/`?` within a segment and `**` as an entire segment. Absolute paths, traversal, backslashes and unsupported glob syntax are rejected. `src/**` overlaps a future `src/new.ts`; `src/**` and `tests/**` are disjoint. Automata prove containment and disjointness; an exhausted complexity limit fails closed. No lane exceeds its approved goal scope, and a shared file always has one owner.

## Runtime records, projections and finite turns

These names omit `.json` because `PlanningStore.saveRuntime` appends it. They live only under the configured `<state-checkout>.runtime`:

- `goalRuntimeFilename(goalId)` → `goal-workflow-<goalId>`: `GoalRuntimeRecord` holds assignment projection, brief, plan, lane progress, final report, events/cursors, redirects, failure and updated time.
- `teamRuntimeFilename(teamId)` → `team-workflow-<teamId>`: `SchedulerRuntimeRecord` holds approved queue with rank/scope/blocking goals, active dispatches, events/cursors and redirects.
- `productRuntimeFilename(teamId)` → `product-workflow-<teamId>`: `ProductRuntimeRecord` holds the Product queue, own-post delivery intent, events/cursors and failure.

`projectGoalRuntime` returns a detached UI projection with goal/seat status, owned files, lanes and PRs, decisions/follow-ups and unowned files. Surfaces consume this API instead of private Developer journals. These new records do not replace historical runtime files.

`WorkflowEvent` is a finite union: startup, proposal, proposal-vetted, approval, developer-report, CI, review, merge, build-running, redirect, goal-closed, seat-idle, retry, queue-changed, conflict and agent-completed. Startup has `{kind, teamId, at}`; other events also have an id for deduplication. CI/review/merge/conflict events bind immutable heads. Delivery is external; services must not sleep or poll in their turn. `DeveloperTurnService`, `ProductTurnService` and `SchedulerTurnService` accept explicit contexts and return records/events. The Scheduler lane owns CLI event ingress and replacing historical timer loops. Product and Developer consume finite turns without importing another lane's private implementation.

`ProductSeat.turn(event)` and `proposeGoals(services,event)` are no-op stubs returning `disabled`; they do no model work, post, approve or write. CLI seat dispatch recognizes an explicitly configured Product before calling `loadDeveloperSeat`. Existing `{kind: "seat", seatId}` tmux routing stays intact. The contract's historical planning bridge reads and verifies Product approvals but does not execute new-model goals while feature lanes are absent.

## Release and archival gates

Human approval happens only on the proposal. `planning merge` is retained temporarily as a compatibility retry, with no new human authorization journal; Surfaces removes M. `planning rollback` remains. Integration, revert and archival merges require a current-head `satori-miyamoto` approval, no outstanding changes requests, nonempty passing required CI, and an immutable head match. They request `--auto --squash --match-head-commit`, then verify the actual merge. Because this repository does not require review in branch protection, an unverified auto-merge request is disarmed before returning so a later unreviewed head cannot merge unattended. Future events trigger a fresh complete verification.

`RunningReleaseEvidence.mergeVerification` stores the verified head, review commit, bot identity and green checks. Historical `approval`/`mergePostId` remain readable. New running-release adapters supply actual bot/CI verification and preserve running build equality/ancestry checks. Retro still verifies every delivered fragment against frozen content, restricts the PR to its own document, reviews the exact head independently and verifies the archival merge before closing.

It preserves all history and does not advance a stage, invent approval or retro evidence, rewrite integration status or discard assignment facts.
